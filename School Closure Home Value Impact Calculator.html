What the research found

The numbers behind this calculator, from studies of real closures and boundary changes.

Hamilton, Ontario
Sales 2005–2017, around actual closures
2–15% penalty · 7.29% per km

Homes lost value in every quarter from six before a primary-school closure to seven after. Each extra kilometre from the nearest elementary school cost 7.29% of value (1.43% per km for high schools). When Prince Philip Elementary closed in 2014, homes in its old attendance area lost up to 9% — over $4 million collectively, by the researchers' estimate more than the school board saved.

Source: McMaster University / University of Toronto research, published in Geographical Analysis — McMaster summary

Michigan
2021 study
13% decrease

A Michigan State study associated school closure with a 13% decrease in residential property values. A separate Michigan Land Use Institute analysis of Jackson found homes within a half-mile of an open elementary school appreciated about 3% faster per year than a comparable neighborhood whose school had closed.

Source: summarized by Reaching Higher NH

Cedar Falls, Iowa
2012 closure · Isakson et al. 2015
6.8% decline

When the Malcolm Price Laboratory School — the University of Northern Iowa's lab school — closed suddenly in 2012, homes inside its attendance zone lost 6.8% of value, about $9,000 at the mean price, measured with a difference-in-difference study. Read with care: it is a single-school study of a university lab school, not a typical public school — Cedar Falls families could choose between the lab school and the city schools, and that choice shaped who lived near it. It is also a conference paper, not a peer-reviewed journal article.

Source: Isakson, Hans R., et al., “Impact of the Closure of a Laboratory School on House Prices,” Hawaii University International Conferences, January 3–6, 2015

Atlanta area
2023, Real Estate Economics
±3% on reassignment

When an attendance-boundary change gave a home access to a substantially better school, its sale price rose about 3% and it sold roughly a month faster. Being moved to a worse-performing school pushed prices the other way — the mirror image of what a closure does to the homes left behind.

Source: 2023 study in Real Estate Economics, via Realtor.com

How the estimate works

The distance model

Estimated change = added distance to school (km) × rate for that school level
  • Elementary: 7.29% per added kilometre — Hamilton hedonic estimate.
  • High school: 1.43% per added kilometre — Hamilton hedonic estimate.
  • Middle school: 4.36% per added kilometre — the midpoint of the two published rates above; no separate middle-school coefficient was published in these studies, so treat this one as the roughest of the three.
  • The distance result is capped at a 15% loss, the top of the penalty range Hamilton observed around actual closures. If the new school is closer than the old one, the model shows a gain instead — consistent with the Atlanta finding that better school access raises prices.

Read this before quoting a number

  • This is an educational estimate built from a handful of studies in specific places and years — not an appraisal, and not a prediction for any individual home.
  • Actual outcomes depend on the replacement school's quality, whether the building is reused, neighborhood demand, and how much of the effect the market has already priced in.
  • The Michigan State figure reaches us through a secondary summary (Reaching Higher NH); the underlying report is not well indexed online.
  • Effects measured in these studies are averages across many homes. Some homes in Prince Philip's old attendance area lost close to 9%; others, less.
